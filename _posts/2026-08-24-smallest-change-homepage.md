title: "Why 'make the smallest change possible' breaks down on a homepage"

When I moved from SEO into a Growth PM role, another growth PM gave me some advice: always figure out what the smallest possible version of a change is, and then do that. The point is to spend as little as possible to find out whether it's a good idea. If it works, you build it out further. If it doesn't, you've lost very little.

I still think that's good advice in most cases, but I also found that there are exceptions.

Early this summer, product marketing at Funnel released the newest version of our product positioning. Web is one of the most important, most visible places positioning shows up, so it was on me and my team to make sure what we say on the website is aligned.

Most of that was straightforward: on every page, replace the old way to talk about Funnel with the new narrative. This was quite easy, especially because I added the positioning doc to a Claude project that I then fed all the web content, and it would let me know which sections on the pages needed updating.

Then I got to the homepage.

We learned over the past year that a homepage can't be updated section by section. It isn't a stack of independent sections, or at least, it shouldn't be. It works best if if tells one clear narrative. Edit it a paragraph at a time and you get a patchwork: sections written at different moments, each anchored to what the positioning or thing we wanted to fix was back at that time.

So instead of updating the old positioning sections one by one, my designer and I stepped back and looked at the homepage as a whole. We rebuilt it as one coherent piece, waited until our product marketer was back from vacation and could weigh in properly, then took it to our CEO as one story instead of a few separate edits.

We got the green light. It goes live very soon.

The smallest-possible-change advice still holds for most growth work: test a new channel, try a different way of explaining the product, or learn whether a feature gets any traction. But it doesn't really work when the thing you're changing is one story that happens to be split across sections, like a homepage or an onboarding flow. Then you're better off doing the whole thing at once.
